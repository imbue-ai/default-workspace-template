- Chat agents can now be shed *before* worker agents once they have gone stale. Previously a chat's memory-shedding band was capped below the worker band, so under memory pressure earlyoom always took a running worker before any chat -- even a chat nobody had messaged in days. That is backwards in the common case: shedding an idle chat costs almost nothing (its transcript stays on disk and readable, and the next message transparently revives it, so the only cost is a slower next message), whereas shedding a running worker destroys in-flight work. The chat band range now straddles the worker band: `CHAT_AGENT_FLOOR` (300, a chat being engaged with right now) through `CHAT_AGENT_BASE` (560, idle but recently used, and still the launch band) up to the new `CHAT_AGENT_STALE_CEILING` (800, untouched long enough to count as abandoned). The ceiling stays below `AGENT_SUBPROCESS` (900), so an agent's own build/test/browser subprocesses are still shed before any agent.

- Engagement and staleness are now combined through a single freshness factor that decays with idle time -- full for the first hour, reaching zero at 24 hours. Engagement bonuses (open tab, visible tab, message recency) are scaled by it, and the remainder lifts the score toward the stale ceiling. The practical effect: a chat with no engagement at all becomes more expendable than a worker after roughly five hours, while an actively-used tab takes most of a day; but presence only *delays* the climb, so a tab left open and untouched for a day still ends at the ceiling. A chat that is mid-turn is exempt entirely -- it is doing work a shed would destroy rather than defer -- and starts aging from when its turn ended.

- The whole schedule is a table of `(idle_seconds, freshness)` points in `bands`, linearly interpolated, so the curve can be reshaped (or made non-linear by adding points) without touching any logic.

- The browser band now holds only the processes that actually hold a browser's memory. The `browser` supervisord program is the *coordinator* -- the daemon that launches and drives Chromium -- and it was being tagged at `SHARED_BROWSER` (1000), the absolute ceiling, which made it the highest-scoring process in the whole workspace and therefore earlyoom's first victim in every memory-pressure episode. Shedding it frees almost nothing (measured: a 125 MB coordinator next to a 1069 MB Chromium tree, none of which was released when it died), and supervisord restarts it straight back into the same session, so it acted as a lightning rod that absorbed sheds without relieving pressure. It now gets an ordinary `SERVICE_BANDS` entry (70, the most expendable built-in service) and `supervisord_program_band("browser")` resolves to that rather than to the browser band.

- `shared_browser_oom_score_adj` now scales Chrome's self-assigned gradation against its real range (0-300, `CHROMIUM_SELF_ASSIGNED_MAX`) instead of 0-1000, so a renderer lands at the ceiling rather than at 937. Under the old scaling every Chromium process was squeezed into the bottom third of the band and the top was left to processes that had merely *inherited* a high value -- which is never a renderer, since a renderer always self-writes.
