A chat left alone for days is now shed along with the browser's tabs, ahead of everything else. A chat used to stop climbing at the stale band (800) a day after it was last used, so under memory pressure a workspace lost its browser, then the commands its agents were running, then the Screen, and only then a chat nobody had opened in weeks. It now goes on climbing on idle time alone, from 800 at one day to 1000 at five: past the Screen's stack at two days, an agent's subprocesses at three, and level with Chromium's renderers from the fifth. A chat that is mid-turn, or waiting on one of its harness's own background tasks, still does not age.

`bands` is laid out as its types, the tables that fill them in, and the functions that read the tables. The chat policy's separate tunables become two tables: `CHAT_STAGES`, the stages a chat left alone passes through (for each, the idle time it is reached at, its band, and how much engagement still counts), and `CHAT_ENGAGEMENT`, what each engagement signal is worth. The browser band's three constants become `BROWSER`. Every name other code reads (`CHAT_AGENT_BASE`, `SHARED_BROWSER_FLOOR`, `SERVICE_BANDS`, and the rest) keeps its value.

How long a chat must be left to count as aged is the one idle time on the `aged` row of `CHAT_STAGES`. `chat_agent_oom_score_adj` takes the stage table as an optional argument, so a schedule can be tried without editing the module.
