`with_agent_env.sh`'s "every cron job should be prefixed with it" rule now names its one exception: the built-in `update-apply-recover` guard the bootstrap writes at each boot carries its own `PATH` line and `cd` instead. The wrapper rebuilds the agent environment from `/home/user/.mngr/env` (with `jq`) and exits non-zero when that is missing, which is exactly the state a workspace left mid-update can be in -- so the one entry whose job is to repair that state cannot depend on it.


The wrapper now exports the full system `PATH` (`/usr/local/sbin`, `/usr/local/bin`, `/usr/sbin`, `/usr/bin`, `/sbin`, `/bin`, after `/root/.local/bin`) rather than prepending one directory to whatever cron handed it. cron gives its jobs a bare `PATH` (typically `/usr/bin:/bin`), so anything installed under `/usr/local/bin` -- `latchkey` among them -- was unresolvable from every scheduled job for the life of the job: on one workspace a refresh job had failed every one of its ~5,600 runs since creation, all on `latchkey: No such file or directory`, while exiting 0 and keeping its last good copy. The list is the one the bootstrap already gives the recovery cron entry.
