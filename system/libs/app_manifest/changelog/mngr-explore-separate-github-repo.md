`load_manifest` reads a manifest tolerantly by default: a key this template does not know, at any depth, is logged as a warning and ignored, so an app installed from someone else's repository still loads when its publisher runs a newer template. `mode=ManifestReadMode.STRICT` keeps the old behaviour, and `app-manifest validate-manifest`, the authoring check, uses it.

A manifest can now say how the app runs and what it needs: `version` and `verified_template`; `[run]` (the main program's command, arguments and environment, which may carry `{port:main}` and `{host}`); `[[services]]` (background programs, each run as `<name>-<role>`); and the guidance an adopter reads, `[[permissions]]`, `[llm]`, `[environment]`, `[[dependencies]]`, `[[adaptation]]` and `[publish] exclude`. All are optional, so existing manifests are unchanged.

The footprint of an app the workspace built or installed (one with a record under `system/config/apps/`) is read from its record and its own directory. Its references are the record's, held to the rules of a manifest reference, and `[[references]]` and `[wiring]` in its manifest are not read; each `[scope] exclude` glob is relative to the app's directory; and its record, its generated program files (one per `[[services]]` entry too) and the link to each skill it carries are part of the footprint. `app-manifest references --for-path` names the app for a path its record lists, for the link to a skill it carries, and for any path under that skill's real directory, and `skill:<name>` for such a skill is the app's footprint. `select-tests` runs the app for a change to its record or to a skill link, and selects no suite of its own for an app with no `pyproject.toml`. A built-in app's footprint is unchanged.

An app with a record is no longer held to the `[[references]]` its manifest may still carry, so an installed app loads whatever its publisher left in; `app-manifest validate-manifest` refuses `[[references]]` and `[wiring]` in such an app, whose record and `[[services]]` say the same things.
