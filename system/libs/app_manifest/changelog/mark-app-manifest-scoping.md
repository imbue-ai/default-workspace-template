A manifest may declare what belongs to its app outside the app's own folder: `[[references]]` (each a literal repo-root-relative `path` plus an optional one-line `note`) and `[scope] exclude` (gitignore-style globs), backed by the `AppReference` and `ScopeRules` models and the `ReferencePath` / `ExcludeGlob` / `ReferenceNote` / `RepoRelativePath` primitives, with duplicate reference paths rejected as a cross-field rule. `load_manifest` takes a keyword-only `repo_root` and, against it (given, or derived from a `system/apps/<package>/app.toml` layout), checks that every reference exists and names neither the app's own directory nor another app's. The new `app_manifest.scope` module turns a manifest plus `system/supervisord.conf` into a creation's footprint -- its own paths, the supervisord sections it owns, its references with a `kind` derived from the prefix, the conventions for its type, the built-in plus declared excludes, and, against a diff base, the changed files that the footprint does not account for -- and reverses the link with `find_referencing_manifests`. Two CLI subcommands expose it: `app-manifest footprint <manifest> | --for-path <path>` writes the scope file, and `app-manifest references --for-path <path>` prints the apps that claim a path. Adds a `pathspec` dependency for the exclude matching, since the standard library's `PurePath.full_match` is neither available on the 3.12 CI interpreter nor gitignore-compatible. Phase 1 of app manifest scoping (`docs/system/blueprint/app-manifest-scoping/`). A `[wiring] programs` table names the supervisord programs an app owns beyond its own and its `<name>-<role>` sidecars (the browser declares `xvfb`), and the first label of every standalone program is a reserved app name.
