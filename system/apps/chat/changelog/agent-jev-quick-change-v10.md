Experimental: before delivering each message sent to a chat, the chat app asks Jev (TypeSafe's decision model) two questions in one call: is this a quick look-and-feel change to an app, and does it touch work the agent has not yet said is done. A quick change to finished work is not delivered to the chat's agent; it goes word for word to a workspace-wide quick-change chat running on Sonnet, which edits the live app under the current editing lease, refreshes its window, and reports in one or two sentences. The chat still shows the user's message, and the report appears as its reply. Anything else, or no answer from Jev within 3 seconds, is delivered as usual. Jev is reached with an OpenRouter key in the chat app's data folder (`openrouter_api_key`) when one is there, else through the account's Imbue proxy. Routed requests and their reports are logged to `quick_changes.jsonl` in the chat app's data folder, and every routing decision with timings to `quick_change_routing.jsonl`.
