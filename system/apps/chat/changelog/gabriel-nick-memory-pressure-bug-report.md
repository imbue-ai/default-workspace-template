The chat app now hands its freed heap back to the OS on a timer, and caps how many allocator arenas it spreads that heap across. A long-lived chat app's memory used to climb for days and only come back on a restart, which is what a user hit when their workspace ran out of memory and the out-of-memory killer started taking their apps and agents.

The growth was not memory the app retains. Replaying an agent stream into the app and taking a census at both ends: Python's own tracked memory stayed flat to within 0.2 MB while RSS climbed 176 MB, and a single `malloc_trim(0)` returned 142 MB of it immediately. Folding the agent stream allocates and frees continuously -- every event arrives as JSON, becomes pydantic models, is folded into the agent view, and is dropped -- and glibc keeps the freed pages in the per-thread arena they came from. So RSS tracked the high-water mark of every arena at once rather than what the app holds.

A `heap-trim` thread now calls `malloc_trim(0)` once a minute (`imbue/chat/heap_trim.py`), and the program's supervisord entry sets `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=4`. Measured by replaying the same 300 seconds of agent stream at each setting: 1.55 MB of RSS per MB streamed as it stands today, 0.77 at `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=4`, 0.69 at 2. 4 is the setting here because 2 costs 55% more CPU per MB against this allocation pattern while 4 costs 9%. Trimming then returns most of what is left: after the capped run, a trim brought the app back to within 39 MB of where it started, against 153 MB uncapped and untrimmed. On a platform with no `malloc_trim` (macOS, musl) the trimmer starts no thread and does nothing.

This is the allocator half of the problem. The app also retains the whole `mngr observe` output stream, which is a separate fix.
