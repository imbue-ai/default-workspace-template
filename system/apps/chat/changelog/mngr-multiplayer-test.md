Chats know who said what (the mngr repo's `specs/multiplayer-refinements/spec.md`, section 5):

- A message from anyone but the workspace's owner (a person the workspace is shared with, or a visiting agent) reaches the agent as `[from: <name>] <text>`, so the agent knows who is asking, on every route that delivers a person's words: the message route, a new chat's first message (the create's and the new-chat intake's, the prefix riding `--message` or following the create, inside the seed context of a seeded chat), and the message that confirms a switch. Slash commands and bash-mode input are delivered as typed. The owner's messages are unchanged. Whether a text is prefixed is decided for the harness it is for (the switch's target while a handoff is in flight, the room being created for a chat with no agent yet, else the active agent's), so a send held while a chat converges into a room lands unprefixed, and a room step that still carries one is shown without it.

- Each chat keeps an `authors.jsonl` beside its transcript, and every `user_message` event the app serves carries `author` (the sender's participant key) and `author_name` when the sender was not the owner. The prefix never shows in the transcript, nor in a send held while the chat switches harness. A send is attributed only once its chat is known (a listed agent, or a chat still being created); a send to an id the app does not know answers 404 with nothing written. A request whose `X-Imbue-Identity` header is present but does not parse is refused with 400 rather than read as the owner's.

- The page draws your own messages on the right as before, and another person's on the left under their name and picture; a turn under either of the owner's keys (`owner`, or the account the roster marks as the owner) is the owner's. The agent's replies are drawn as bubbles, one per reply, under the workspace's picture and name. The page re-reads its own key and the roster when it comes back into view or focus and when anyone's mentions change (a few seconds' cache between), so a page opened before the workspace was shared learns the owner's new key, and anyone who joins later gets their name and colour.

- New routes: `GET /api/me` (the page's own participant key), `GET /api/participants` (the shell's roster, every picture the shell serves -- a person's profile picture, the workspace agent's avatar icon -- pointed at this app's `GET /api/participants/<key>/picture`, which relays it with the shell's caching, since the roster names those pictures on the shell's origin, which the page cannot reach).

- The chat list's rows show a kebab beside the rename pencil on hover, opening the row's context menu.

Chat rooms and mentions (the mngr repo's `specs/multiplayer-refinements/spec.md`, section 7):

- A room is a chat among the people in the workspace with no AI agent. A chat waiting for its first send becomes one from its provider chooser ("Chat room (no provider)"), which its provider chip opens (and its first send does, while nothing is signed in), and any chat (the main chat included) becomes a room, or a room becomes an agent chat, from the composer's provider menu: the switch keeps the conversation so far as the chat's earlier segments, and a chat leaving a room hands its new agent the room's transcript. Rooms sit in the chat list with the other chats, wearing a people glyph, and take every chat verb (rename, delete, stop, start). A message sent into a stopped room is appended to its transcript without reviving its reader, so the room keeps reading as stopped until `mngr message --start <room>` (the send `AGENTS.md` has the agent make; a bare `mngr message` refuses a stopped agent), the row's Restart, or boot starts the reader again.

- A room is an mngr agent of the template's new `room` type: its process reads what `mngr message <room>` types into its pane into the room's transcript, which `mngr transcript <room>` renders and the chat app tails. The chat app appends people's messages to the same transcript, so every message in a room is drawn as its author's turn under their name and picture, the owner's unmarked.

- Typing `@` in a room's composer opens a list of handles: the people here, `@agent` for the workspace's agent, and every chat by its title (`@Main-chat`). Mentioning `@agent` or a chat from a room (a room's first message included, once the room is up) sends that chat the message, marked as said in the room, and the room's row shows a working dot until the agent answers into the room (a chat that refuses the message, as one whose account was signed out does, is not waited on; the wait is also let go when either chat is destroyed, and once five minutes have passed since the ask it is dropped the next time the chat list is computed, whether or not a page had the room open). A mention in a send no proxy vouched for (an in-workspace script's) acts on nothing. Mentioning a person marks the room's row with an `@` for them until they look at it (the owner's pending mentions are read and cleared under both of the owner's keys; a destroyed chat's mentions are dropped from everyone's file, which is named by the key itself), and tells the workspace's owner through the Imbue Studio app's feed as the room itself, so the notification opens the room. In an agent chat a handle is a word the agent reads, and the composer offers no popover.

- The chat snapshot carries `kind` (`agent` or `room`) and `awaiting_chat_ids`; `POST /api/chats/create` and `POST /api/chats/<id>/handoff` take `kind` (a room is refused beside an account or a model, before anything is written); `GET /api/mentions` and `GET /api/mentions/pending` are new, with the `mentions_updated` push on `/api/ws`. There are no room-only routes, no room store, and no second chat id shape.

The chat app feeds the desktop's tray and makes the Lounge (the mngr repo's `specs/multiplayer-refinements/spec.md`, sections 6 and 7.5):

- Every message a person or a visiting agent sends through a route a proxy vouched for (a new chat's first message included; an in-workspace script's send, such as a background task's report, is nobody's speech and raises none), and every turn the workspace agent closes with text in a chat some page is reading (its post into a room included; the turns reach the feed through the session watcher a page's stream builds, so a chat nobody has open raises none), is posted to the shell as a speech bubble (`POST /api/bubbles`, with the people a room message mentions), and every chat-list change is posted as what each chat is doing (`POST /api/chats-activity`), both best-effort and off the request thread; the last chats-activity report is posted again once a minute, so a shell restarted since paints the agent's card. A secondary chat posts neither.

- The Lounge is the chat titled "Lounge", of either kind, found by title alone, by the rule a create refuses a taken name by (canonical forms compared case-insensitively, so "lounge" and "Lounge " are the Lounge too; a Lounge converted into an agent chat, or a chat so named, is the Lounge). A room under that title is made the first time the roster holds anyone besides the owner (a person, an invited email, or a visiting agent), from the roster the shell answers (read once a minute), and again when a click on a person needs the Lounge after it was deleted or renamed, only while no chat of any kind carries the title. The new `lounge` intake target resolves to it, making the room when there is no such chat, and the manifest's `lounge` launch path (`mention_param = "message"`) drafts `@<handle> ` into it when a person's tray entry is clicked.

- The README's intake paragraph names the `lounge` target and every launch path the manifest declares, and the manifest's comments on the `main`, `new`, and `send` paths name the key each answers (`main` is the launcher's Enter, `new` its Ctrl+Enter, `send` is bound to none, as `draft` is).

The chat app's browser test of a desktop switch drives the shell's overview (the taskbar's "Manage Desktops" button and its thumbnails) rather than the desktop switcher the shell no longer draws (the mngr repo's `specs/multiplayer-refinements/spec.md`, section 8.2); nothing in the chat app itself changes.

The chat about an app (the mngr repo's `specs/multiplayer-refinements/spec.md`, section 9.2):

- The manifest declares `app-chat` ("Chat about this app", `POST /api/chats/intake`, params `app` and `app_display_name`, preset `target = "app_chat"`, `app_params = {name = "app", display_name = "app_display_name"}`), which a window's title bar balloon runs; never a launcher row.

- The new `app_chat` intake target answers the chat labelled `app_chat` with the `app`'s registered name (so a renamed app keeps its chat), else the one titled "<app_display_name> Chat" by the rule a create refuses a taken name by (canonical forms compared case-insensitively, so a chat titled "docs chat" by hand is found rather than the create refused beside it), of either kind or still being made, else creates one with that title and label on the default account (never auto-named), else, with nothing signed in, mints one awaiting its first send under them (`mint_awaiting_chat` takes a name and labels). The intake is always held, even with nothing sent, as a draft that prefills the composer with "Change something about the <app> app: " (the lead-in that tells the agent which app the message is about, prepended over anything already typed, never sent by itself), with a `hint` ("How do you want to change <app>?") on the pending intake's view and its apply answer, which the chat root puts on the chat's composer as its placeholder and focuses (`setComposerHint`, the page's embed API's `hintComposer`). An applied intake with nothing to draft or launch now answers `null` for `composer_text` and `first_message` rather than an empty string. The chat app's own windows open "Chat Chat".

- The chat's pinned entry defaults to the bar (the manifest drops `default_mode = "floating"`): the avatar sits in the tray among the people, and a client floats it from the entry's menu.

- A room never answers to an account label: the workspace's create defaults (`.mngr/settings.local.toml`) stamp the default account's label and `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` onto every create, so a room's create clears both for its own invocation (`-S commands.create.label=` and `-S commands.create.env=`, with mngr's `MNGR__ALLOW_SETTINGS_KEY_ASSIGNMENT_NARROWING` opt-in in that create's environment, since mngr otherwise refuses an assignment that narrows a list), and every reader of the label answers no account for a room regardless, so a room's snapshot names none, a sign-out of that account never refuses the room's sends, and a sign-in's restart sweep never bounces it. A seeded chat launched as a room appends its first words alone, with no seed context.

- Chat bubbles take the prototype's look: the agent's turns sit in the same 24px-gutter column as another person's (the workspace name over the run's first bubble, the face beside it, a spacer holding the gutter under continuation groups), a bubble that is not yours (a person's or the agent's) wears one 5% grey, the viewer's own a green tint, and every bubble an 18px round with a 4px corner squared toward the sender. The chat list's selected row wears the same grey as a not-yours bubble, its rows' hover a fainter 3%.

- Every delivering route annotates every sender a proxy vouched for -- the owner's page sends included, while a request with no identity header (a script's, the terminal's) still writes nothing -- and each chat's snapshot gains `participants`, the distinct annotated senders in order of first message (never the workspace agent). The chat list's rows show their pictures when two or more have written, stepping aside under the pointer where the pencil and the kebab come out.

- The chat root's relay forwards its nested pages' `shell:input` activity reports up to the shell, like their focus.

- A monogram (a person with no picture, or one whose picture would not load) sits on the person's roster colour, as the desktop's tray draws it.

- The terminal behind a chat attaches as a viewer (read-only, never sizing the owner's window) while the shell's handshake says the chat's window is being looked at on somebody else's desktop: the back face's pty URL carries the viewer argument after the agent name, which is always sent (empty for the ambient session).

- `system/scripts/message_chat.py` says a room is answered with `mngr message --start <room>`, never through the script, since a script's words are nobody's.
