Hovering a window behind the focused one and scrolling now scrolls its page, with no click first. A click or press on a background window reaches its page and brings the window to the front in the same press, as in a native desktop application; before, a transparent cover took that first press and only raised the window. Keys typed after the press go to the window that came forward.

The shell raises a window whenever the document's focus lands in its page, so this holds for every app, whether or not it speaks the shell contract. It reads where the focus is when its own window loses it and on a timer every 100 ms (a tick), so a press that moves the focus from one page to another raises the window within a tick. A focus the shell reads within two ticks of a message it just sent the page (it is shown, or a terminal is asked to take the focus) is no press to the shell, which hands it back: a page that sends no focus report of its own for it, such as a terminal-like page focusing its nested frame after a reload, a desktop switch or an agent's refresh, stays behind the window you are in. A page that reports its own focus (`shell:focused`, sent by the first-party pages when their window gains focus) raises its window regardless. The focus requests the shell posts to a raised window are dropped once another window is focused. A click that a page cancels, such as one in the chat while its own menu is open, does not move the focus, so that window comes forward on the next click. After the shell raises a window on seeing a focus landing it raises no other that way for three ticks, so pages that take the focus back whenever they lose it no longer flip the stack on every press.

Touch screens keep the cover over background windows, so the first tap there still only raises the window, and every window is covered while a menu or the launcher is open, so the press that closes them never reaches a page.
