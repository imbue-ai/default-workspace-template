A new avatar design the shell draws itself, as a body with physics rather than a picture: it can be poked, it goes restless while there is work, and it stands up when you are at its window.

- A new avatar, the imbue character, alongside the drawn designs in the chooser. Every other design is an SVG the shell serves as an image and animates through a shared stylesheet; this one is the imbue mark running as a rig in the page -- one filled path, no outline, no face -- which is what lets it answer a press. It is listed, chosen and stored exactly like the rest; only the drawing of it differs, and only in the browser.

- Its shape is the mark's own, not a redrawing of it. The "i" dot was traced and fitted as a sum of harmonics, and the two artefacts of the stem that dot sits on -- a real concave dent where the neck begins, and a bottom extrapolated out past where the drawing ever showed it -- are blended back to the shape's own ellipse, since the character is a head and nothing else. What is left is convex the whole way round, and the part that was actually measured is untouched.

- Pressing it dents the body where the pointer landed and gives a little under the press, then springs back. Nothing is keyframed: a press sets a target or throws an impulse at a spring and the follow-through falls out of the physics, which is why the same gesture reads right whether it lands at the top, the side, or underneath.

- While any agent on the machine is working, its surface never quite settles: a shallow dent somewhere, held, let out, pressed somewhere else. Nothing marks the moment work starts, because what drives that mood crosses at an instant you have no reason to recognise -- a state that is always a little unsettled can be coarse and still read true, where a gesture at the wrong moment cannot.

- It draws itself up out of its resting slant when you are actually at its window -- focused, not minimized, not pulled out into a window of its own -- and slouches back when you leave. Standing up goes past true vertical, since the body's own long axis runs up and to the right and at zero rotation it still reads as leaning. The lean is the whole of the response: you arrive by clicking the character, and a body that hops out from under the pointer that just pressed it fights the press rather than answering it.

- The avatar no longer fades when its window is minimized or pulled out. On an app's icon that fade reads as "not on screen"; on a character it reads as unwell, which is a mood nobody chose. The entry around it still shows the state the way it always did.

- The avatar's hover bubble appears above it rather than below. It floats just over the taskbar, so the shared below placement was landing the bubble on the bar.

- Reduced motion gets the character, held still. The other designs answer that preference by stopping their animations; a rig has to be told, so it is run forward to its resting pose and then frozen there -- the resting shape comes from the idle wander, and a body with that switched off relaxes to a plain oval it never actually holds.

- The chooser's preview, and anywhere else that needs a picture rather than a rig, gets a still of the resting pose generated from the rig itself, so it cannot drift from the pose the character holds.
