Rewrote the chat transcript's scroll virtualization so reading history no longer makes the viewport jump.

The transcript reserved scroll space for unloaded history at a fixed 160px per *event*, but the renderer groups a whole turn into one row -- a tool-heavy turn of 50 events renders as a single progress block. Each backfilled page therefore landed thousands of pixels shorter than its reservation in one frame, which unmounted the row the browser was anchored to and teleported the viewport.

Space is now reserved from real measured geometry. Each row's height is measured once after it settles, remembered per conversation and viewport width, and reused: revisiting a conversation is accurate immediately, and ranges this client has never rendered are estimated from what it has actually measured rather than from a fixed constant.

Scrolling the bar deep into a long conversation now lands where the thumb says it should, and spurious jumps -- fired when estimate drift pushed the position calculation past its threshold -- no longer happen.

Those measurements are now kept by the workspace rather than by one browser, in a small file beside the stored tab names and recency. A conversation reopened in another window -- or after a reload, or on another device at the same width -- reserves its space from what was already measured instead of measuring from scratch. The server only stores and returns what a client measured: it never derives a height itself, drops rows that are not measurements rather than failing the write, and keeps only the most recently read transcripts, so the file stays small and a dropped one is simply measured again. A write that names no measurement at all stores nothing and clears whatever was held for that width, so it can neither push out a conversation someone is reading nor give an unmeasured workspace a file. A conversation long enough to exceed the per-transcript row limit keeps its newest turns, which is where reopening it lands.

Rows that have not been measured yet are also sized from what the transcript actually renders at, rather than from a fixed height per kind of row. A generic estimate is necessarily wrong in the same direction for a whole conversation -- every assistant row is guessed at the same height whether it holds three words or a screenful -- so on a conversation of short messages, scrolling up rendered a screenful of rows that each shrank by a couple of hundred pixels the moment they were measured. The content collapsed under the reader, the bottom rose to meet them, and the transcript decided they wanted to follow new messages again and pulled them down to the end.
