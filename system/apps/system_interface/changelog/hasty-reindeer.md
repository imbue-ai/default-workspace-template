Dragging a shortcut around the desktop is a different gesture now. Before, the icon you pressed faded to a fifth of its strength and stayed in its cell while a copy of it followed the pointer, and a dashed rectangle drew itself over the cell you were about to drop into. The copy, the fade, and the rectangle are all gone. The icon you pressed **is** the one that moves: it lifts out of its own cell at full strength, grows a tenth, casts a deeper shadow, and tracks the pointer exactly, with nothing eased so it never lags behind the hand.

What tells you where it will land is the shortcut in the way getting out of it. Hold the icon over a cell another shortcut is in and that shortcut steps aside, live, under your hand, and steps back if you move on. Hold it over an empty cell and nothing else moves at all: the gaps you leave between your shortcuts are yours, and closing them up behind a drag would be the desktop rearranging itself rather than making room. Nothing but the shortcut you are dropping onto ever moves -- a shortcut that merely happens to sit between where you picked the icon up and where you are dropping it is left alone.

Where it steps to is unchanged: the nearest free cell, ties settled by the lower column then the lower row, which is what the desktop has always done on a drop. The cell the icon in your hand is leaving counts as free, so a short drag often just swaps the two. What is new is only that you see it happen before you let go.

Nothing at all is written while you are still holding the icon -- what you see under your hand is the desktop showing you the result, not saving it. The drop is one request naming the shortcut you dragged, exactly as before; where the displaced one goes is the workspace's own answer, the same rule the drag drew. Both cells are drawn the moment you let go rather than after the round trip, so there is no flicker back to where they started, and the icon settles into the cell your hand left it in rather than sliding there from the cell it was picked up in.

Two smaller things came with it. The cursor is an open hand over a shortcut and a closed one for as long as one is in your hand, wherever on the desktop the pointer has got to -- the icon under it has been lifted, so its own cell is no longer what the cursor is over. And a drag redraws the desktop only when the pointer crosses into a new cell, since that is the only thing that changes what the other shortcut is doing; the lifted icon's own travel is written straight onto it, a frame at a time.

Dropping a shortcut back into the cell it came from now asks the shell for nothing, where before every drop wrote a move whether or not anything had changed.
