The desktop's menus are the workspace's shared Menu, and its own menu module goes.

`views/Menu.ts` is deleted, with everything it restated: its `placeMenu` and the anchor types (`workspace_ui/menu-position` has them, with `above` besides), its `FloatingCard` (no caller but its own Menu), and its `MenuItem`/`MenuEntry`/`MENU_DIVIDER` row vocabulary (the shared row types). `anchorForEvent` and `anchorForPoint` move to `menu-position` beside the `MenuAnchor` they make.

The five menus -- a window's, a taskbar entry's, a shortcut's, the Desktops tray's, and a desktop's -- are ONE `createMenu` in the App. The App still says which menu is open and what it was opened for, and its rows follow from that; opening, placement, Escape, the invisible sheet and dismissal are the component's, and its `onClose` is what keeps the two from drifting apart. `WindowMenu.ts` builds `MenuRow[]` (`windowMenuRows`, `taskbarEntryMenuRows`) with the same rows, order, labels and actions as before.

The trigger exceptions go with it: a press on the button of an open menu lands on the menu's sheet and closes it there, which is the toggle those callbacks were written to produce. Dismissal is a press on the sheet or Escape rather than a document `pointerdown` listener, and the cards take the shared 16px radius and the `--z-popover` layer. A test finds a menu by its own class (`.window-menu`, `.entry-menu`, `.shortcut-menu`, `.desktops-menu`, `.desktop-menu`) and a row by `data-menu-row`, in place of `data-floating` and `data-menu-item`; the Desktops tray button drops the `desktops-menu` class it wore beside its `data-desktops-menu` hook, which now names the menu it opens rather than the button.
