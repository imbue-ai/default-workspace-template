- Restyled the in-chat permission-request card: a muted "Permission request" eyebrow now tops the card, followed by a lock badge beside a short title ("Local files", "Other machines", "Device accounts", or the resolved service name) with the agent's rationale directly beneath it. The pending card pairs a solid green "Review & respond" button with a plain-text "Show raw request" toggle, and resolved requests render as a compact one-line receipt ("Approved" in green, "Denied" in neutral, "Couldn't complete" in amber).

- The card no longer lists the specific requested permissions inline (the old "Requesting" line and its hover tooltips); those details live in the review modal and the raw-request disclosure.

- The card flips to Approved/Denied the moment the request is resolved in the Minds app's review popup, rather than waiting for the resolution to come back through the agent transcript. The verdict arrives over the minds embed contract (the same postMessage path in the desktop app and in a plain browser), which admits it only from this page's own embedder and only with a well-formed payload; the transcript's own resolution still wins once it lands.

- All messaging with the embedding Minds app now goes through that shared contract module rather than hand-rolled postMessage calls, so both ends of the boundary ship from one source of truth.

- The in-chat permission card no longer loses a still-pending request to output truncation. A latchkey permission-request response is often longer than the transcript's per-result output limit — the gateway pretty-prints the created request and echoes its target and precomputed effect, and curl's progress meter is printed ahead of it — so the response was being cut mid-object and the card fell back to an apologetic "couldn't read this request" line for a request that still needed an answer. The parser now finds and parses the request before truncating, carries it on the tool-result event as structured data, and keeps the object whole in the raw output, so the card shows the request's name and its "Review & respond" button every time.

- A card whose transcript predates that fix now recovers what survived the cut rather than giving up on the whole request: the request's name, the agent's rationale, and a working "Review & respond" button all come back from a truncated response, and any field that did not survive is simply left off instead of guessed at. When nothing at all can be read the card says so in one short line rather than a paragraph, and the "Show raw request" disclosure stays available in every state.

- The in-chat permission card now leads with a key rather than a padlock, and its badge shows the app being asked about — Slack's own mark on a Slack request, Gmail's on a Gmail request. A service we ship no artwork for, and a request that names no app at all (local files, other machines, device accounts), fall back to a neutral cube. The marks are the same artwork the Minds app's Permissions screen uses, read from the vendored mngr tree at build time rather than copied in, so the two surfaces cannot drift and the card looks the same embedded in the Minds app and opened directly in a browser.

The permission-request scan over tool output is now bounded: a probe cap and a RecursionError guard keep a pathological response (kilobytes of braces, or absurdly nested JSON) from stalling transcript parsing or killing the session watcher outright — measured, a 100KB brace wall dropped from ~3s to ~3ms. The chat card's recovery-scan comments now also give the scan's real trigger (a response past the backend's preservation ceiling, since an agent's rationale has no length limit), and a test pins that case end to end.
