The New Tab page is an ordinary tab now. As many can be open as you ask for, in one pane or across panes, and one stays put until it is closed or answered -- where before exactly one could exist in the whole dock, and it folded away the moment any other tab took focus. The "+" is offered whether or not the pane already holds one.

Three things enforced the old rule and all three are gone: the focus handler that removed every launcher in the dock as soon as an instance panel was activated, the branch of the open that focused and flashed the launcher already in the target group instead of adding a second, and the "+" hiding itself while its group held one. Two helpers and a module-level flag went with them, having had no other callers.

One narrowing stays, and it is per pane: a New Tab alone in a pane stands for the pane, so the next tab docked there takes its place. That is what keeps an emptied dock tidy -- the dock is never empty, so closing the last tab mints a New Tab to stand in for it, and opening anything spends it -- and it needs nothing to recognise a stand-in by, because a New Tab you opened yourself and left alone in its pane means the same thing. What the other panes hold has no bearing on it. A New Tab sharing its pane with another tab is left alone, and so is a pane holding several. The shell applies the rule to an agent's document ops too, so `layout.py open` behaves as a click does: a pane showing nothing, or one New Tab, is filled rather than split beside, while a pane of several New Tabs is split beside like any other.

Opening something from inside a New Tab still answers that tab, and now leaves the result **where that tab stood**: the placement carries the launcher's own index within its strip and the launcher is retired after, so with three New Tabs up, opening from the middle one leaves you in the middle. Previously the panel was appended and the launcher removed afterwards, which shunted the result to the end.

The New Tab's tab glyph is a dashed squircle rather than a plus -- an unfilled outline for the one tab that is not showing anything yet. At the 14px a tab glyph renders at, the corner radius stays well under half the side (a dashed rounded rect loses its corners first and reads as a circle from about 6 up), `pathLength` restates the perimeter as 64 so the dashes fall in eighths and the path closes on a dash rather than a part-gap, and the rect sets `butt` against the file's `round` linecap, which would otherwise add half a stroke width to each dash and swallow the gaps.

The project rail gets out of the way of what it puts on screen. It expands on hover and floats over the dock, and it used to stay open until the pointer happened to leave, so opening something from it left the thing you just opened underneath it; only one case closed it, clicking a tab row that already had a tab. Now every pick that puts a tab on screen collapses it -- a shortcut row, a tab row whether or not it was already docked, an All apps row, and the two shortcut-menu entries that open or focus an instance. Picks that act on the rail itself (pin, unpin, rename, a mode flip, an app's Stop or Start) leave it open, since there is nothing new to look at, and so does switching views, which changes what the rail lists rather than opening a tab. Entering the rail again brings it back.

A collapsed rail offers nothing to aim at: no hover highlight, and no row buttons. Collapsing leaves your pointer parked over a rail that has changed shape under it, and a row's buttons sit at its right edge, which in the narrow rail is on top of the icon you just clicked -- so the three dots slid under the cursor and lit up as though you had reached for them. Hovering the rail is what opens it, so the only way to be over a collapsed row is a pick that just collapsed it.
