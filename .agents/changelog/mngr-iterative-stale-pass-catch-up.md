A background pass whose base moved before it could land is now caught up instead of thrown away. When another chat commits while an update-self, update, heal or critical-app worker runs, the lead keeps the worker and asks it to merge the current `HEAD` into its branch. The worker resolves any conflict there, regenerating lockfiles rather than picking a side. It hardens what came in and re-runs its test gate over only what the catch-up changed (`select-tests --diff-base <its tip before the merge>`), then reports `done` again. Before, every such case discarded the finished pass and dispatched a new one from scratch; for update-self that meant redoing a roughly 40-minute validation because of an unrelated commit. A fresh pass now replaces a stale one only when its worker is gone.

update-self in particular: before the apply, the lead waits for other chats' in-progress edits (a dirty tree, an `editing service` or `editing critical app` lease) to settle instead of refusing. It then checks whether the worker's branch still fast-forwards, and catches the worker up if not. The worker guide has a new §7 for the catch-up. The catch-up merge must not carry an `update-self:` subject, and the worker does not re-run its full 4b selection, which would re-select the whole pass. The exit-1 guidance in SKILL.md, `apply-outcomes.md` and the apply's own refusal message now say to catch the worker up rather than re-dispatch, and never to destroy the waiting worker. That instruction is what led a lead to destroy a finished, validated worker.
