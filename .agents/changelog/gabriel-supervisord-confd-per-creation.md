The `build-app` scaffolder writes the app's supervisord program to its own `system/supervisord.conf.d/<name>.conf` instead of appending to the shared `system/supervisord.conf`, and no longer registers the app in the root `pyproject.toml` at all. Scaffolding two apps now produces only new files, so two agents can build two creations in one workspace without one blocking the other's hardening pass.

The scaffolder's port pre-flight and its duplicate-name guard both scan every drop-in as well as the main config. Without that the pre-flight would have handed a new app port 8081, which the `browser` service holds in its own drop-in.

Scaffolded programs run `uv run --all-packages <name>`: with no root dependency entry, a root-closure-scoped `uv sync` prunes the member and deletes its console script, and `--all-packages` reinstates it on the restart that follows.

`harden-contention.md` now states that a creation's footprint is exclusively its own, and carves generated lockfiles out of the "any merge conflict means a stale pass" rule. `uv.lock` is regenerated by every scaffold and marked `merge=binary`, so without the carve-out two concurrent creations would conflict there as a matter of course and force one into a full re-harden -- reintroducing, at the merge step, exactly the serialization this layout removes.

`github-sync` writes its own drop-in rather than appending to the shared config; `update-self` classifies `system/supervisord.conf.d/**` as a service-class path; and `migrate-workspace` scans the drop-ins when collecting the source's and this workspace's app ports, which it needs to detect collisions before a migration.
