`service-processes.md` describes the `agent-observer` program: the workspace's one `mngr observe`, a manifest-less supervised service with no app directory, which every chat instance follows and which a chat never starts on its own.

The build-app scaffold writes the `[preview]` table into every new app's `app.toml`: the library's default for the name spelled out (`<PACKAGE_UPPER>_PORT`, `_HOST`, `_DATA_DIR` over a copy of `data/.apps/<name>`), so an edit to the runner's env names has the table to keep in step beside it.

`update-app` boots any app's preview from its manifest: `preview_app.py up --app <name> --worktree <dir> [--with <sibling>]... [--instance-key <key>]` reads the worktree's `app.toml`, resolves its `[preview]` table, boots the siblings first, writes the shell's registry copy with each sibling's row pointing at its preview, and surfaces the app as the labeled `<name>-preview` tab; `refresh` re-boots the inner process in place after a rebuild and `down` tears the preview and its siblings down -- including after a boot that failed with siblings already up, whose record is kept so they stay tearable-down rather than being stranded. One preview per app at a time: a different worktree's live preview is refused rather than hijacked. The skill's isolated-instance section points at it for any app with a manifest and keeps the raw `serve_isolated_instance.py` invocation for manifest-less services.

`serve_isolated_instance.py` carries the old live-editing branch's work forward and gains what the preview table needs. Carried: `refresh --name` re-boots the inner server on its existing port after waiting for the old process to release it, recording the new pid before the health wait; `down` escalates SIGTERM to SIGKILL and removes the state directory only once every recorded process group is gone, keeping the state and exiting non-zero for a survivor (`up` refuses to boot over a stale instance it could not clear); a failed boot quotes the log since the last boot marker and the health probe's response body, and copies the log to `data/.state/isolated-instances/<name>-failed.log`; both the inner server and the wrapper launch through `oom_tag_service.py user`. New: `--port-env NAME=ENVVAR` and `--port NAME` for named free ports, `--copy KEY=SOURCE` for a directory copied into the instance's scratch space, `{port:...}`, `{copy:...}`, `{scratch}`, and `{host}` filled in the launch argv and `--env` values, and `--inner-path` for the page the preview frame opens (`preview_wrapper_server.py --inner-path`). The state file records ports, copies, and the inner path, so `refresh` and `down` need nothing but the name.

`update-app` routes by manifest. Its first step finds the app the change touches and reads its `app.toml`: `critical = true` (the shell, the chat, the terminal, and any user app that declares it), or a change under `system/libs/workspace_ui/`, follows the new `references/critical-app.md`, the careful flow that replaces the `update-system-interface` skill: one `editing critical apps` lease held from entry through go-live or abandonment, an isolated worktree provisioned in the background, the live loop through `preview_app.py up`/`refresh` and `layout.py open <name>-preview` (what to point the user at per app, the screenshot fallback when no client can show the tab), the two handoff shapes to a harden worker created at approval on the lead's branch, the freshness check over the app, the shared library, and the npm lockfile, the apply with both bundles and `--keep-rollback-point`, the exit codes, the notice only a person closes, and the teardown. A pass on an app whose notice is still open proceeds and says the new apply replaces its rollback point. The skill's verify step says to verify before the user can see it, the turn-end section drops the system-interface pointer, and the isolated-instance section says to refresh in place rather than re-`up`. `update-system-interface` and its `reveal_system_interface.py` are deleted.

`update-self`'s apply keeps a rollback point when asked. `update_self.py apply --keep-rollback-point` (the careful flow's flag; update-self's own applies never pass it) leaves its snapshots in place on success and writes `data/.state/update-apply/last-good.json`: the merge it landed, the tree it landed on, the copies it kept, the critical apps whose program or bundle it changed (read off the merged tree's manifests: a changed path under the app, a bundle owner when the frontend was rebuilt, the shell when its process's code changed), the supervisord programs a rollback restarts, and whether the diff reached the workspace's own setup (the provisioner, `system/Dockerfile`, the bootstrap). Any later apply replaces the record and its copies. Two subcommands close it: `rollback-last` forward-reverts the merge (work committed since is kept), restores the copies, re-reads supervisord's table when it changed, restarts exactly the recorded programs (or, when the diff reached the workspace's own setup, restores the files and names `mngr start --restart system-services` for an agent), waits for the workspace to settle, refreshes every open view, and writes its progress and outcome into the record for the shell's notice; `confirm-last` discards the copies and the record. A settled record is refused a second rollback and closed by `confirm-last`; a failed rollback probe takes the apply's existing emergency exit, and a rollback that dies in a way nobody predicted settles the record with what failed (keeping the copies) rather than leaving the notice reading as a rollback still in flight, which refuses every verb. The apply, its recovery, and the rollback now share one "settled" verdict: several consecutive healthy answers from the shell and every critical app's instances API on supervisord pids that hold still across all of them, a pid that turns over restarting the count rather than failing it.

`launch-task`'s `create_worker.py launch` and `launch-sync` take `--branch <spec>`, passed through to `mngr create`, so a worker can check out and extend a branch the lead already built up; `launch-sync` reads the worker's branch back from `mngr ls --format json` (`initial_branch`) rather than assuming `mngr/<name>`, from the payload rather than the exit code, fails fast and destroys the worker when it cannot, and validates the worker name before interpolating it into the CEL filter.

`update-creation`'s `type` is `skill`, `app`, or `service`; a critical app is `type: app` reached only from the careful flow, its task carries no `## Change origin` marker (stated where the task-file format is defined), and its go-live belongs to the careful flow. `heal-creation` says a heal of a critical app is a live edit at the agent's own risk and points at the careful flow for the safe path; both spell `create_worker.py await` with its required `--name`.
