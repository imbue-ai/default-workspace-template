`build-app` now shows a new app inside a labeled frame for as long as it is a mock, so the user never mistakes hard-coded content or a sample of their data for the finished app. The frame is the preview wrapper with a "UI Sketch" banner ("early UI sketch -- not the finished app") and a note under it, written by the agent, saying what is not real yet ("Showing 20 of your emails as a sample; refresh doesn't work yet."). It stays up after the user confirms the mock and comes down only once the app reads its real data source: a `fetch-process-show` surface rendering the confirmed `sample.json` is still framed until it is pointed at the pipeline's output.

`build_app_skill/mock_frame.py up --app <name> --note "..." --open|--refresh` frames the app's live program as the `<name>-sketch` app ("<display name> (UI sketch)"), booting nothing else, and re-registers the app itself as internal (out of the launcher) and exempt from the no-window stop (only the frame has a window). The frame records its note and page, so `mock_frame.py refresh --app <name>` needs nothing else: while a frame is up it re-frames the app as recorded and reloads the mock window, which is what puts the hidden registration back after the app restarts (`verify_app.py --restart ... && mock_frame.py refresh`) or the workspace reboots; with no frame up it reloads the app's own windows. `up` without `--note` keeps the current note, and re-framing reuses the wrapper's port, so an open mock window keeps its origin. `mock_frame.py down --app <name>` closes the mock window, removes the frame, and restores the app's registration. `crystallize-creation`, `heal-creation`, and `update-creation` reload an app `build-app` made with `mock_frame.py refresh`, and `update-app` does so while the app is still framed.

`preview_wrapper_server.py` takes `--kind change|mock` (default `change`, the existing preview banner; `mock` is the "UI Sketch" banner) and `--note`. `serve_isolated_instance.py` gains `frame()`, the wrapper boot and registration around a service something else runs, which `down` tears down like any instance, and `read_frame()`, what a frame was booted with.
