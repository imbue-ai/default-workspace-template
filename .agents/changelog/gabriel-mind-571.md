`build-app` now shows a new app inside a labeled mock frame for as long as it is a mock, so the user never mistakes hard-coded content or a sample of their data for the finished app. The frame is the preview wrapper with a "Mock" banner ("early mock -- not the finished app") and a note under it, written by the agent, saying what is not real yet ("Showing 20 of your emails as a sample; refresh doesn't work yet."). It stays up after the user confirms the mock and comes down only once the app reads its real data source: a `fetch-process-show` surface rendering the confirmed `sample.json` is still framed until it is pointed at the pipeline's output.

`build_app_skill/mock_frame.py up --app <name> --note "..." --open|--refresh` frames the app's live program as the `<name>-mock` app, booting nothing else, and re-registers the app itself as internal (out of the launcher and off the desktop) and exempt from the no-window stop (only the frame has a window). The app's next start registers it normally again, so `up` is re-run after each `verify_app.py --restart`; re-running it re-boots the wrapper on the same port, so an open mock window keeps its origin. `mock_frame.py down --app <name>` closes the mock window, removes the frame, and restores the app's registration.

`preview_wrapper_server.py` takes `--kind change|mock` (default `change`, the existing preview banner) and `--note`. `serve_isolated_instance.py` gains `frame()`, the wrapper boot and registration around a service something else runs, which `down` tears down like any instance.
