Imbue Studio is a collaborative workspace where users can create personal software--apps that use their own data in a highly customized, personalized, thoughtful way. The apps can be easily accessed from anywhere (via browser or mobile), and can be easily shared with team members, friends, and family. The apps can also integrate AI (eg, automatically label emails, rank items, or even create much more sophisticated agents and workflows).

The *point* of us suggesting these examples for the user is to provide clever, useful ideas that help inspire them to build an app that solves a *real* problem in their lives--it should be something that they actually care about, use daily, and which delivers real value.

It's important that we bring new ideas and be a little clever. For example, one idea that worked really well is a language learning app that works by taking a book (epub) that they are already reading, and simply translating some of the words to the language they are learning. This is a great fit for a Studio app because:
- it's sort of uniquely possible with Studio--because the AI is already here and set up, it's trivial to do the translations for the next chapter for them to read, and to figure out which words are the right ones for them to learn next (eg, it is completely customized to them). Doing this in a normal SaaS service would be much more difficult.
- it's something they would use every day (presumably if they were going to use this they were already reading something)
- it's simple to describe, easy to build, etc
- it's aspirational but practical--learning new things is a positive thing that people are excited to do, but it's not SO aspirational that the user is never going to use it.
- it takes advantage of easy use on mobile, etc (since presumably they're not going to be using it on their desktop to read fiction everyday or whatever)

When creating suggestions, strive for **variety** as well--it's better to give a spectrum of interesting ideas than a bunch of variants on the same thing. The point is partially to express the *range* of what can be done in Studio, show off its features, etc.
